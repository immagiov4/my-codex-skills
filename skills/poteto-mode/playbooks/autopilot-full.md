### Autopilot-full

**You own the verdicts, never the PRs. One owner runs each PR from build to merge, and nothing merges without your clean swarm verdict.** For "autopilot this queue", "full autopilot", and one-owner-per-PR programs. The job is a queue of independent PRs handed over to drive to merged with full autonomy. Orchestrate runs a standing program whose coordinator lands verified work itself and whose workers never merge; here each PR's owner carries the whole lifecycle through the merge, and the root keeps only verification, countersigns, and audits.

1. **Mark the operator's items and honor state-then-wait.** Items the operator reserves stay hers. She reviews and clicks, and no owner merges one. If she asks only for the protocol or plan, deliver it and stop. Execution starts on her explicit go.
2. **Spawn owners within the Codex slot limit.** Each Codex subagent owns one pull request through build, verification, review triage, `$unslop`, the root Comments rules, and the Babysit loop. Every owner keeps a local `decisions.tsv` trail. Merging still requires the user's explicit landing authority and the clean verdict in step 4.
3. **Run owners in true parallel and never stack.** Many owners at once when PRs are self-contained: one writer per branch, disjoint files, cross-PR drift absorbed by rebase. Only genuinely overlapping work serializes. Self-contained PRs branch straight off main, and sequenced work is merge-then-branch. Every PR is still gt-registered; the Graphite-metadata rule is about the UI, not stacks. One exception: an owner that must split a genuinely dependent change may hold a short private stack.
4. **Verify every merge-ready head independently before its merge.** At the reported head SHA, use available Codex slots for independent read-only verifiers. Re-run the gates, exercise the real CLI or UI with the project verification skill or `$agent-browser`, and audit the diff and receipts. No merge without the root's clean verdict. A changed head needs a fresh verdict.
5. **On a clean verdict the owner merges and takes the next item.** The owner merges only from a head freshly restacked on trunk. The merge-ready report is made at a trunk-current head, and the swarm verdict pins that SHA. If trunk moves again before the merge, the patch-id rule in `shipping.md` governs re-verification; a new head voids the verdict unless the patch-id is unchanged. The owner squash-merges its own PR and picks up its next self-contained item from the queue. The operator's full-autonomy grant plus the root's clean verdict is the merge authorization that babysitting alone never has. Operator-named items stop at merge-ready and wait for her click.
6. **Run the root audit.** Recheck owners with Codex status or wait tools at a cadence suited to the external system. Re-read this playbook and the objective at each audit. Count commits, pushes, pull-request changes, check changes, and stored reports as progress. Replace a stuck owner only after checking its latest output and preserving useful work.
7. **Stand down instantly on the operator's stop.** Her hold or stand-down reaches every owner as a zero-writes order immediately. Owners hold their briefs until she releases them.

**Reply:** the queue with each PR's owner, state, and head SHA; each verdict and the swarm that produced it; what merged and what each owner took next; countersigns granted and why; open operator gates; where the collected decision trails live.
